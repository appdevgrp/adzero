Privacy at AdZero

Last updated 19 November 2026

AdZero is an ad and tracker blocker for iPhone, iPad and Android, published by D.K. BARAK BOOK KEEPING LTD. This page describes what the app does with information, in plain terms. When we say “we”, we mean the publisher.

What happens to your network traffic

To filter ads, AdZero asks your phone for permission to add a VPN profile. It uses the operating system’s standard tools for this — Network Extension on iPhone and iPad, VpnService on Android. Despite the name, this VPN has no remote end: it is a small local filter that runs on the phone itself.

The filter is set up so that it only ever sees domain name (DNS) lookups:

  • The phone is told to send its DNS lookups to an address that exists only inside the filter. That address is the only thing routed into it.
  • For every lookup, AdZero compares the requested domain with the lists shipped inside the app and with the sites you have allowed.
  • If the domain belongs to an ad or tracking service, AdZero immediately replies that the domain does not exist. That lookup goes nowhere.
  • Any other lookup is passed on to the public DNS service you picked, and its answer is handed back to the app that asked.

Pages, videos, downloads, messages and every other kind of traffic travel over your usual connection without entering the filter. AdZero cannot read them, and it never learns full web addresses or what is inside a connection. The blocking lists are part of the app; it does not download them or anything else from us.

Public DNS services

Lookups that aren’t blocked still have to be answered by someone, so AdZero sends them straight from your phone to one of three public DNS services. Like any server your phone talks to, the service you choose receives the domain being looked up and your phone’s public IP address.

We have no contract with them and get nothing from them; how they treat the lookups is set by their own terms, so it’s worth reading them before you switch. AdZero sends lookups in regular, unencrypted DNS format, which means your internet or mobile provider can also see them — exactly as it could without AdZero. You can change the service in Settings → DNS server while protection is off.

What the app keeps on your phone

AdZero saves a small amount of data in its own private storage on the device. On iPhone and iPad this storage is shared only between the app and its filter extension. We never receive any of it.

  • Preferences — the DNS service you picked, your allowed sites and, on iPhone and iPad, whether Always on is enabled.
  • Lookup counts — how many lookups happened and how many were blocked on each day. Days older than 30 days are discarded automatically.
  • Today by the hour — how many lookups were blocked in each hour of the current day, replaced every new day.
  • Top blocked domains — names of blocked domains with a count, up to 100 per day over the same 30 days.

Domains that were allowed are counted but their names are never written down. The statistics hold no IP addresses, no web addresses and nothing about which app sent a lookup. The VPN profile itself lives in your phone’s system settings.

On iPhone and iPad this data can end up in an iCloud or computer backup that you make, the same as any app data; those backups are yours and Apple’s, not ours. On Android, AdZero opts out of system backups.

Things we never collect

Since there is no AdZero server, none of the following reaches us:

  • DNS lookups or browsing history
  • Statistics from the app
  • Name, email or phone number
  • IP address or location
  • Contacts, photos or files
  • Advertising ID or device IDs
  • Usage analytics or crash logs
  • Payment details

AdZero has no in-app purchases, sends no push notifications and does not sync to any cloud. If you have opted in to sharing diagnostics with developers in your phone’s settings, Apple or Google may pass us anonymous crash reports and aggregate usage figures through their developer consoles. We look at those only to fix bugs.

Sharing and selling

We don’t hold personal data, so there is nothing for us to sell, rent, share or use for advertising or profiling. The only outside parties involved are the DNS service you choose, as explained above, and Apple or Google as the app store and operating system provider, whose own policies cover their services.

Removing your data

  • Wipe the statistics with Reset statistics, found in Settings and on the Statistics screen.
  • Manage exceptions in Settings → Allowed sites.
  • Delete the VPN profile from your phone’s VPN settings at any time (iPhone and iPad: Settings → General → VPN & Device Management; Android: Settings → Network & internet → VPN).
  • Uninstall AdZero to erase everything it stored, together with its VPN profile. Older backups you created keep their copy until you delete them.

There is nothing about you on our side to erase. Questions about lookups already sent to a DNS service should go to that service.

Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR in the EU and UK or the CCPA/CPRA in California give you rights over personal data a company holds about you — for example to see it, fix it, take it elsewhere, have it erased or object to how it is used, as well as to complain to a data protection authority. We are happy to answer any such request, but in practice we will have nothing to hand over, because the data AdZero creates exists only on your phone, where you control it directly. We do not sell or share personal information as the CCPA defines those terms, and using your rights will never affect how the app works for you.

Children

AdZero is not aimed at children under 13, or under the age of digital consent where you live. We don’t knowingly receive personal information from anyone; if you think a child has sent us some, write to us and we will delete it.

Keeping data safe

Filtering happens on the device and only unblocked lookups leave it. Stored data sits in the app’s private storage, out of reach of other apps and covered by the phone’s own encryption. No system is perfectly secure, so we also suggest keeping a passcode on your phone.

Updates to this page

If AdZero changes in a way that affects this policy, we will revise this page and its date. Any change that would make the app collect information will be announced in the release notes or inside the app before it applies.
